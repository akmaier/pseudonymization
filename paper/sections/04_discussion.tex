\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

For releases prioritising leakage reduction, the measurements support the large union with its
specificity cost reported. On CARDIO:DE, detector agreement raises specificity from 0.869 to 0.986 but
reduces sensitivity. On Enron, two votes lower person sensitivity from 0.890 to 0.732 and raise
fixed linkage fivefold. Across these tested configurations, lower sensitivity coincides with greater
name exposure and linkage.

Attack results depend on adversarial knowledge and denominator. The
corpus-internal prior is an upper bound, while the public prior estimates risk under one available
source; their difference constitutes the frequency result here. Corpus-level scores must therefore
be accompanied by document, case and language results.

The measurements support choosing replacement form for utility. The placeholder release is
computable from the surrogate release, so a scheme-aware adversary can obtain the same privacy
exposure from either. Telling the ranker the scheme raises recovery from 0.110 to 0.165 on unchanged
text. Token-level tasks survive surrogates but fail with \texttt{[PERSON]}; document-level utility is
higher with placeholders.

The prediction that sensitivity moves frequency alignment and linkage in opposite directions is
refuted for these attacks, priors, corpora and ensemble. Better detection was expected to starve
context linkage while enlarging the population available for frequency alignment. Frequency
alignment recovered no identity at any tested sensitivity with these priors, while both linkage
attacks declined as sensitivity rose. In these experiments, missed identifiers limited both
measured attacks.

Linkage is measurable only on Enron and CARDIO:DE because TAB and
OntoNotes link mentions within documents. People recur there, so the reported linkage values
understate rather than bound their risk. CARDIO:DE names were inserted into an already de-identified
release, making its frequency distribution synthetic and its clinical realism partial. Dates pass
through by design, leaving that channel open. The candidate-ranking attack presents ten names and
therefore represents an adversary with a shortlist, not population search. Every number describes
one release under one key.
