\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Safe sharing of clinical databases requires both modalities to be anonymised. A protected image
does not compensate for a report that names the patient, and a protected report does not remove
identity from the image. Artificial intelligence makes both tasks possible at scale, but learned
failures must be measured at the level at which data are released.

For text, we price detection, utility, and re-identification on the same documents. Detector
precision governs utility more strongly than the choice between realistic surrogates and typed
placeholders. Better detection protects against context linkage while strengthening the frequency
signal available to another attacker. Aggregate token recall, in turn, understates exposure at the
document and patient levels. Hence, there is no single anonymisation score. A release should state
its operating point, language-specific errors, surviving cases, attack model, and stability. The
operating points and the measurements behind them will be released with the paper.
