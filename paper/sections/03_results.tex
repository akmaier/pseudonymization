On CARDIO:DE, the fast configuration gives up 0.097 sensitivity while raising specificity from
0.9670 to 0.9769 at 191 times lower cost. Enron cuts cost from \SI{25.557}{s} to \SI{0.057}{s} per
message. The OntoNotes specificity rows coincide because the \SI{10}{\percent} band admits all 414
candidates.

Within OntoNotes, language effects depend on what is detected. Under the recommended ensemble,
sensitivity is 0.989 on English, 0.868 on Chinese and 0.614 on Arabic; pooled sensitivity is 0.801.
Arabic supplies \SI{45}{\percent} of identifier tokens from \SI{7}{\percent} of documents, so it
dominates the pooled result while remaining the hardest case. Person sensitivity transfers better
at 0.991, 0.932 and 0.878, pooling to 0.935. The thirty-seven-point spread therefore lies in the
other identifier classes rather than the people targeted by the attacks.

\subsection{Utility}\label{sec:results-utility}

Detector precision governs utility more strongly than the replacement form. Named-entity agreement
on CARDIO:DE is $0.611\pm0.082$ with surrogates under the permissive rule and $0.973\pm0.019$ under
the precise rule; with placeholders it is $0.004\pm0.008$ and $0.674\pm0.082$. The placeholder
figures measure the metric as much as the release, because the frozen recogniser never tags
\texttt{[PERSON]} as a name, so every replaced mention counts as a disagreement and the comparison
is informative between rules rather than between forms.

The other two tasks carry their original-text scores and admit the paired test. Medication
extraction scores $0.371\pm0.182$ on unmodified text; under the precise rule it moves to
$0.364\pm0.189$ with surrogates, a paired difference of $-0.008\pm0.178$ not distinguishable from
zero ($q=0.80$), and under the permissive rule it falls to $0.346\pm0.181$, a difference of
$-0.020\pm0.182$ that is ($q=5.6\times10^{-3}$). Section classification scores $0.749\pm0.093$ on
unmodified text and loses $0.072\pm0.086$ to surrogates against $0.038\pm0.089$ to placeholders,
both significant ($q=1.6\times10^{-37}$, $q=2.6\times10^{-14}$; rank-biserial $-0.846$, $-0.531$).
At document level, typed placeholders preserve more utility than realistic surrogates. At token
level, only surrogates survive.

With this frequency attack and public name list, no identity is recovered on the four corpora.
Every returned name was missed by the detector and printed in clear text, so it measures defender
sensitivity rather than adversarial inference. The corpus distributions, unavailable to a real
attacker, align at most one identity. Raising the abstention threshold from $\varphi=0$ to
$\varphi=0.05$ leaves 11 of 25,677 CARDIO:DE queries and none elsewhere; alignment $c@1$ is zero at
every threshold. These results concern this attack and these priors, not safety against other
attacks.

Linkage requires cross-document identity and must be read against unmodified text and chance. On
Enron, fixed similarity recovers 0.706 of held-out people from unmodified text and
$0.0095\pm0.0012$ after the recommended release. The latter is 36 times the $1/3816$ chance rate
but two orders of magnitude below the unmodified-text rate. Entity-disjoint training raises recovery to
$0.0407\pm0.0029$, four times the fixed attack and 155 times chance, so a trained adversary carries
the residual risk. CARDIO:DE has 207 gallery identities; fixed linkage recovers no identity, and
learned linkage $0.0071\pm0.0160$ against chance $0.0048$ is statistically indistinguishable from
guessing.
Across the tested Enron configurations, linkage rises as person sensitivity falls. Agreement
between two detectors lowers sensitivity to 0.732, raises fixed linkage fivefold to 0.0500 and
increases clear-text names from 180 to 1,868; intersection yields 0.037 sensitivity and 0.2380
linkage.

The third attack initially assigns different risks to the replacement forms. Ranking one marked
mention against ten candidates, a language model falls from 0.995 on unmodified TAB text to
0.110 with surrogates, near the 0.100 chance rate, but reaches 0.340 with typed placeholders;
CARDIO:DE (0.085 against 0.120) and OntoNotes (0.290 against 0.630) order the same way. Read as protection
the ordering suggests stronger surrogate protection, but it reflects ranker behaviour. Both
conditions replace identical spans. Overwriting each surrogate span with \texttt{[PERSON]}
reproduces the placeholder release character for character, verified on all 1,268 TAB and all 5,994
OntoNotes documents. Anyone able to locate those spans can compute the placeholder release from the
surrogate release. A scheme-aware adversary with unchanged text reaches 0.165; neutralising
name-like spans with a public list reaches 0.145, against 0.340 for the rewrite. For this candidate
ranking attack, the direct surrogate score measures the ranker's acceptance of plausible names,
while both forms carry the higher reachable risk.

Sensitivity hides exposure, and the denominator decides what a number means. The recommended
release leaves one CARDIO:DE person mention of 4,396 in clear text, one entity of 1,957 and one
patient of 270; over every identifier type, \SI{8.5}{\percent} of letters carry something and
\SI{12.2}{\percent} of patients do. TAB leaves a name in 44 of 1,268 judgments and OntoNotes in 736
of 5,994 documents, while Enron leaves one in \SI{18.0}{\percent} of its people and something in
\SI{45.8}{\percent} of its messages.

\label{sec:results-stability}%
The key normaliser both merges and splits identities: on Enron, ORGANISATION surrogates collide at
\SI{17.3}{\percent} and drift at \SI{48.6}{\percent}, and PERSON drift reaches \SI{37.7}{\percent}
against \SI{5.0}{\percent} on CARDIO:DE. The lower CARDIO:DE figure reflects constructed recurrence
with fewer natural variants rather than generalisation.
