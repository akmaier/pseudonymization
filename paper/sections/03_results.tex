The fast point is usually not the worse one: on CARDIO:DE it gives up 0.097 sensitivity but is more
specific than the maximum, 0.9769 against 0.9670, at 191 times less cost, and Enron repeats this at
\SI{0.057}{s} against \SI{25.557}{s} per message. The two OntoNotes specificity rows are one
ensemble, because the \SI{10}{\percent} band admits all 414 candidates.

Language is a separate axis, and how separate depends on what is detected. Over all identifier types
the best triple reaches 0.983 on English, 0.710 on Chinese and 0.545 on Arabic, and the pooled 0.745
describes none of them: Arabic supplies \SI{45}{\percent} of the gold tokens from
\SI{7}{\percent} of the documents, dominating the pooled score while being the hardest case. People
transfer far better, at 0.991, 0.932 and 0.878 with the recommended ensemble, pooling to 0.935, so
the forty-point spread belongs to the other identifier classes rather than to finding people, which
is what the attacks try to do.

\subsection{Utility}\label{sec:results-utility}

Detector precision governs utility more strongly than the replacement form. Named-entity agreement
on CARDIO:DE is $0.611\pm0.082$ with surrogates under the permissive rule and $0.973\pm0.019$ under
the precise rule; with placeholders it is $0.004\pm0.008$ and $0.674\pm0.082$. The placeholder
figures measure the metric as much as the release, because the frozen recogniser never tags
\texttt{[PERSON]} as a name, so every replaced mention counts as a disagreement and the comparison
is informative between rules rather than between forms.

The other two tasks carry their original-text scores and admit the paired test. Medication
extraction scores $0.371\pm0.182$ on unmodified text; under the precise rule it moves to
$0.364\pm0.189$ with surrogates, a paired difference of $-0.008\pm0.178$ not distinguishable from
zero ($q=0.80$), and under the permissive rule it falls to $0.346\pm0.181$, a difference of
$-0.020\pm0.182$ that is ($q=5.6\times10^{-3}$). Section classification scores $0.749\pm0.093$ on
unmodified text and loses $0.072\pm0.086$ to surrogates against $0.038\pm0.089$ to placeholders,
both significant ($q=1.6\times10^{-37}$, $q=2.6\times10^{-14}$; rank-biserial $-0.846$, $-0.531$).
At document level the realistic surrogate is therefore not merely unnecessary but worse than the
typed placeholder, while at token level it is the only form that survives.

Frequency matching recovers nothing. With a public name list it names no identity on any corpus;
every name it returns is one the detector missed and the release printed in clear text, which
measures the defender's recall rather than the adversary's inference. Even the corpus's own
distribution, which no real attacker holds, aligns at most one identity, and raising the abstention
threshold from $\varphi=0$ to $\varphi=0.05$ makes the attack decline all but 11 of 25,677 CARDIO:DE
queries and all of them elsewhere, so the alignment's $c@1$ is zero at every threshold. This holds
for one attack and these priors and does not establish safety against others.

Linkage succeeds where identity is real, and needs two anchors to be read. On Enron the fixed
similarity recovers 0.706 of held-out people from unmodified text; after the recommended release it
recovers $0.0095\pm0.0012$, still 36 times the $1/3816$ chance rate but two orders of magnitude
below that ceiling. Training the metric on entity-disjoint examples raises it to $0.0407\pm0.0029$,
four times the fixed attack and 155 times chance, so the residual risk belongs to an adversary who
can learn. On CARDIO:DE, with 207 identities in the gallery, the fixed attack recovers nothing and
the trained one $0.0071\pm0.0160$ against chance $0.0048$, not distinguishable from guessing. Recall
bounds this: two detectors agreeing lowers Enron person recall to 0.868, raises fixed linkage
fivefold to 0.0500 and takes the names in clear text from 180 to 1,868, while intersection replaces
almost nothing and leaves linkage at 0.2380.

The third attack shows why the replacement form cannot be credited with a difference. Ranking one
marked mention against ten candidates, a language model falls from 0.995 on unmodified TAB text to
0.085 with surrogates, the 0.100 chance rate, but reaches 0.290 with typed placeholders; CARDIO:DE
(0.090 against 0.135) and OntoNotes (0.310 against 0.590) order the same way. Read as protection
this says surrogates defend better, and that reading is wrong. The conditions replace identical
spans, so overwriting each replaced span of the surrogate release with \texttt{[PERSON]} reproduces
the placeholder release character for character, verified on all 1,268 TAB and all 5,994 OntoNotes
documents. The placeholder release is therefore computable from the surrogate release by anyone able
to locate those spans, and no release is safer than a text derived from it: the gap measures a
ranker that believes the surrogate, which is obscurity, not protection, and both forms carry the
higher risk.

Token recall hides exposure, and the denominator decides what a number means. The recommended
release leaves one CARDIO:DE person mention of 4,396 in clear text, one entity of 1,957 and one
patient of 270; over every identifier type rather than people alone, \SI{8.5}{\percent} of letters
carry something and \SI{12.2}{\percent} of patients do. TAB leaves a name in 44 of 1,268 judgments
and OntoNotes in 736 of 5,994 documents, while Enron leaves one in \SI{18.0}{\percent} of its
people and something in \SI{45.8}{\percent} of its messages: person recall 0.890 and nearly half
the messages carrying an identifier describe one release.

\label{sec:results-stability}%
The key normaliser both merges and splits identities: on Enron, ORGANISATION surrogates collide at
\SI{17.3}{\percent} and drift at \SI{48.6}{\percent}, and PERSON drift reaches \SI{37.7}{\percent}
against \SI{5.0}{\percent} on CARDIO:DE, whose lower figure reflects constructed recurrence with
fewer natural variants rather than generalisation.
