\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Detection}\label{sec:results-detection}

Table~\ref{tab:operating-points} is the cost--quality front. It retains all four operating points
for each corpus so that an inexpensive system is never compared with a maximum-quality system from a
different denominator. Enron's entries are provisional: its detection sweep is being repeated after
a correction to the combination rule, and the ensembles it selects may change.

The fast cell is usually not the worse cell. On CARDIO:DE it gives up 0.097 sensitivity, yet it is
\emph{more} specific than the maximum---0.977 against 0.967---and 191 times cheaper, detecting
\SI{89.5}{\percent} of the placed identifiers in \SI{0.30}{s} per letter. Note that the maximum buys
its last points of recall by over-detecting, and the specificity column is where that cost appears.
On Enron the same pattern is starker still: \SI{0.06}{s} per message against \SI{25.6}{s}, for 0.09
of sensitivity. Across the comparison, cost spans three orders of magnitude while quality changes by
less than a factor of two. Yet a different ensemble wins on each corpus, so no detector recipe
transfers unchanged.

Language is not a detail. OntoNotes token sensitivity is 0.983 for English, 0.710 for Chinese, and
0.549 for Arabic. The pooled value, 0.747, describes none of them. Arabic supplies
\SI{45}{\percent} of the gold tokens although it supplies only \SI{7}{\percent} of the documents.
This imbalance makes a pooled score both numerically dominated and operationally misleading.

Consensus also removes whole classes. When eight votes out of 15 are required, DEMOGRAPHIC, MISC,
and QUANTITY fall
to zero on all three applicable corpora. CODE loses \SIrange{94}{99}{\percent}, and Arabic
detection falls to \SI{1.7}{\percent}. The apparent precision gain is therefore not a uniformly
better detector. It is a decision to stop detecting difficult classes.

\subsection{Utility}\label{sec:results-utility}

Table~\ref{tab:utility} collects the comparisons that distinguish detector precision from
replacement policy. Under realistic surrogates, named-entity agreement is 0.611 with the permissive
union but 0.973 with the precise rule. The condition and surrogate inventory are identical. Only
the detector spans change. TAB and OntoNotes reproduce this ordering in paired per-document scores.
Hence, detector precision dominates replacement policy for this measure.

    \midrule

    \bottomrule

Surrogates matter when the output depends on the individual tokens. Typed placeholders collapse
entity agreement to 0.004 under the permissive rule, because every person becomes the same string
and the tagger has nothing left to align. Yet at document level the advantage disappears, and in
fact reverses: section classification scores 0.677 under surrogates and 0.711 under placeholders.
Note that a classifier reading a whole letter does not care which name stood where, so the machinery
that constructs a realistic replacement buys nothing there. Medication extraction is the clearest
case of the same point: under the precise rule the difference from the original text is not
statistically distinguishable from zero.

There is an important caveat to the high agreement. Under the precise rule,
\SI{98.3}{\percent} of Arabic identifiers and \SI{93.3}{\percent} of Chinese identifiers are never
replaced. The downstream model therefore sees nearly the original text. Calling this utility
``preserved'' without the paired detection result would reward a privacy system for doing little.

\subsection{Leakage}\label{sec:results-leakage}

Recall changes the population available to an attacker. For frequency matching, raw top-1 accuracy
falls as detection recall rises because the candidate pool grows. When we divide by the chance rate
for that pool, the trend reverses: lift rises monotonically from 31 to 75 on TAB and from 48 to 91
on OntoNotes. The denominator must therefore be held constant before attributing a privacy gain to
recall.

The attacks nevertheless disagree, as Table~\ref{tab:leakage} shows. On Enron, context linkage
falls from 0.172 to 0.051 as recall rises, whereas the lift of frequency matching holds. The
unmodified-text ceiling for context linkage is 0.706. Learned linkage is approximately twice as
successful as fixed context linkage throughout, quantifying what training buys the adversary.

\begin{table}[t]
  \caption{Leakage measures move in opposite directions with recall and must be read against their
  own baselines.}\label{tab:leakage}
  \centering\small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}llcc@{}}
    \toprule
    Corpus & Attack and measure & Low recall & High recall \\
    \midrule
    TAB & frequency-matching lift & 31 & 75 \\
    OntoNotes & frequency-matching lift & 48 & 91 \\
    Enron & context-linkage Rank-1 & 0.172 & 0.051 \\
    TAB & LLM Rank-1, A $\rightarrow$ B & 0.999 & 0.087 \\
    TAB & LLM Rank-5, condition B & \multicolumn{2}{c}{0.514} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Rank-1 alone can also overstate protection. On TAB, LLM candidate ranking drops from 0.999 on
unmodified text to 0.087 after pseudonymisation. Yet, Rank-5 remains 0.514. With ten candidates,
chance Rank-1 is 0.100 and chance Rank-5 is 0.500. Condition B therefore reduces this attacker to
guessing inside the stated closed world; it does not establish anonymity outside that world.

Finally, token leakage understates what survives. At maximum sensitivity on CARDIO:DE,
\SI{26.8}{\percent} of letters still carry at least one finding and \SI{73.2}{\percent} are clean.
At patient level, \SI{7.0}{\percent} retain a name in the clear. These denominators answer different
questions: one leaked token, one affected letter, and one exposed patient are not interchangeable.
The full report therefore retains the distribution of surviving findings per document, because one
residual mention and seven give an attacker different evidence.

\subsection{Stability}\label{sec:results-stability}

The stability metrics expose simultaneous under- and over-merging by the key normaliser. On Enron,
ORGANISATION surrogates collide at \SI{17.3}{\percent} while drifting at
\SI{48.6}{\percent}. PERSON surrogates drift at \SI{37.7}{\percent}, compared with
\SI{5.0}{\percent} on CARDIO:DE. The surface-form rule collapses unrelated organisations that share
a short name, yet separates one person written with a full name, surname, title, or initials.
CARDIO:DE's low drift is not evidence that the rule generalises. Its recurrence was constructed and
has fewer natural variants. These collision, fragmentation, and drift measurements therefore need
the provenance of the identity layer beside them.
