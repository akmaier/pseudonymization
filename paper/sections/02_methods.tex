\section{Material and Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Corpora}\label{sec:corpora}

Table~\ref{tab:corpora} states each gold standard's provenance because their recall denominators are
not interchangeable. TAB provides manually annotated identifiers and document-scoped
co-reference~\cite{pilan2022tab}. OntoNotes covers three languages, but was annotated for
linguistics, not privacy. A \emph{named entity} is a mention classified as a person, organisation,
location, or another predefined type. Enron's header-derived gold carries genuine cross-document
identity. CARDIO:DE is the clinical anchor; its gold identifiers were inserted into an already
de-identified release.

Each corpus has one limit that changes how its results should be read. In CARDIO:DE, the gold is
complete only for the identifiers inserted by our construction; moreover, \SI{84.7}{\percent} of
gold tokens are dates that the transformation does not replace. In TAB, the applicant is typically
named once but subsequently occurs as ``the applicant'', with a median of 17 role mentions. We
report OntoNotes separately by language and never pool it. Enron alone provides natural
cross-document identity.

\subsection{Conditions}\label{sec:conditions}

Condition A is unmodified text. In B, each detected identifier becomes a realistic surrogate that
remains stable throughout the corpus. Its index comes from HMAC-SHA256, a \emph{keyed hash}: a
one-way digest that cannot be recomputed without the secret key. In C, the same span becomes an
unindexed typed placeholder such as \texttt{[PERSON]}. B and C therefore differ only in replacement
form. The policy follows European guidance~\cite{enisa2021pseudonymisation} and prior e-mail
pseudonymisation~\cite{eder2019emails}.

Before hashing, we casefold each mention, collapse whitespace, and remove titles and punctuation.
Thus, \emph{Dr. Weber} and \emph{weber} agree, while \emph{Weber} and \emph{Kay Weber} do not. We
hold this rule fixed and examine its failures in Sect.~\ref{sec:results-stability}.

\subsection{Detectors and Ensembles}\label{sec:detectors}

We evaluate 15 frozen detectors: rules, zero-shot and fine-tuned encoders, multilingual and German
clinical recognisers, and eight gateway language models. Cached spans are combined by union,
intersection, or a vote of at least $k$ members over single detectors, pairs, and triples.

Output-token limits require particular care. The cost of a model reply depends on script because
the same visible text may require different numbers of subword tokens. For a reasoning model, much
of that budget is fixed rather than proportional to input length. A proportional budget truncated
\SI{92}{\percent} of Chinese replies. We corrected it and repeated the runs because this otherwise
resembles a detector failure.

\subsection{Operating Points}\label{sec:operating-points}

For each corpus we select a fast and a maximum operating point on sensitivity and specificity.
Sensitivity is the fraction of gold identifier tokens detected; specificity is the fraction of
non-identifier tokens left untouched. The rates use separate denominators. Parallel ensemble cost
is the slowest member's time. The maximum is the best non-dominated configuration; fast is the
cheapest ensemble within \SI{10}{\percent} of it. A sensitivity floor is mandatory, since detecting
nothing maximises specificity.

\subsection{Measurements}\label{sec:measurements}

Detection is scored at token and entity level. An entity is protected only when every mention in
its co-reference chain is replaced. We additionally use information-weighted precision, which gives
more penalty to masking a token that conveyed information not predictable from its remaining
context. This complements recall. Exposure is counted per document, case, and entity; few leaked
tokens can still expose many people.

For utility, frozen models analyse all three conditions. We measure named-entity-recognition
agreement, i.e. span F1 between a model's output on original and transformed text, and two clinical
tasks: medication information extraction and section classification. Continuous paired scores use
the Wilcoxon signed-rank test; binary correctness uses McNemar's test. We control the false-discovery
rate with Benjamini--Hochberg correction within each task family. Original-text performance is
always reported beside it.

Stability has three denominators. A collision occurs when distinct gold entities receive one
surrogate. Fragmentation occurs when one entity receives several surrogates within a document.
Drift occurs when its surrogate changes between documents. These metrics apply to condition B,
where a mapping exists, rather than to a placeholder that deliberately merges all entities of one
type.

\subsection{The Attacks}\label{sec:attacks}

\emph{Frequency matching} aligns surrogate and real-name frequency ranks. It requires a matching
population reference, which CARDIO:DE lacks because we placed its names. \emph{Context linkage}
matches profiles built from words around a person's mentions. It requires other documents about
the same cross-document identities, so it runs on Enron and CARDIO:DE, not TAB or OntoNotes.

\emph{LLM candidate ranking} shows a model one marked mention and ten candidate names, with or
without other text about each candidate. It needs that candidate population and, in the second arm,
auxiliary text. This is a one-in-ten closed world, not a population re-identification rate.
\emph{Learned linkage} trains the context-linkage task on entity-disjoint examples. It additionally
needs labelled training data; its gap to fixed linkage measures the value of learning. Gallery and
query documents are disjoint. A \emph{dictionary lookup} would hash candidate names, but is defined
and not run because no condition is unkeyed.

\subsection{Stability and the Key Normaliser}\label{sec:key-normaliser}

The fixed rule deciding whether surface mentions denote one entity casefolds, collapses whitespace,
and removes titles and punctuation. Note that distinct people may collapse to one key while initials
and full names remain separate. Section~\ref{sec:results-stability} reports both effects.
